# Heavy-lock supervision ownership contract (#657, phase 1)

This is the required contract for the #657 redesign, not a claim that current `scripts/heavy-lock.sh`, `scripts/tdd/heavy-lock.py`, or the lanes already implement it. It governs a **workload invocation**, including its descendants, and the separate Swift, App, hosted App, and repeat evidence consumers. A test process exiting is not workload cleanup. `PICSEE_HEAVY_LOCK_HELD=1` is a nesting hint, not proof of a held descriptor, a supervisor, or cleanup.

> Specify one authoritative owner for each process group, deadline, cancellation and cleanup result. Nested HELD=1 execution stays inside the parent's cancellation boundary, preserves inherited lock descriptors, and cannot extend the parent's deadline. CI's HELD=1 without an inherited lock must have an explicit supervisor. Define invocation-scoped status publication without making a nested lane wait for its own ancestor to exit. `complete` attests to verified cleanup, independently of test success; HELD=1 alone proves neither.

## Terms and invariant

- **Invocation**: one command admitted by a supervisor. It has a unique invocation ID, one monotonic execution deadline, one cancellation boundary, and one status record. A lane attempt and every repeat iteration are distinct invocations. An outer invocation can contain nested invocations.
- **Boundary owner**: the one live supervisor accountable for signalling and verifying a process group (and any registered workload outside that group). It may delegate observation of a nested invocation but not its ultimate cancellation duty. A nested supervisor must not create an untracked session/group that the boundary owner cannot cancel.
- **Lock owner**: the shared, inherited kernel flock open-file description, not a PID or the `HELD` flag. The acquiring supervisor opens and passes the descriptor through every protected spawn; no participant calls `LOCK_UN`, renames, or unlinks the persistent lock file. The lock remains held until the last inheriting descriptor closes. The mdpro directory is a separate cooperative lock and has its own acquisition/release owner.
- **Cleanup proof**: the boundary owner's verified absence of live workload members after leader reap, plus verified cleanup of any registered out-of-group host and auxiliary supervisor processes. A failed or unavailable process query is *unknown*, not empty. A surviving process after KILL is *incomplete*, even if its RSS is zero. The acquiring supervisor retains the mdpro directory when cleanup is incomplete; a surviving inherited flock descriptor still protects the flock. No automatic force unlock follows a heuristic.
- **Deadline**: a finite monotonic timestamp set when the command launches, separate from the lock-acquisition wait. Nested effective deadline is `min(parent deadline, requested child deadline)`. It never resets or extends the parent's budget. The current #650 decision budgets are 5 minutes for discovery, 10 for filtered Swift, 20 for full package, and 30 for coverage/Xcode/asset/unspecified heavy commands; explicit finite per-command overrides are permitted only after preflight validation. Acquisition wait remains 30 minutes. Normal post-exit drain is 30 seconds. At execution timeout, start TERM immediately, then 10 seconds of grace, KILL, and 5 seconds of observation; do not first add normal drain. The outer deadline governs nested cleanup even if an inner grace is longer.

The boundary owner stays outside the group it owns. A normal child exit starts drain verification, not automatic release. Signal cancellation and deadline expiry follow the same bounded escalation. Timeout is 124. Incomplete cleanup after a successful command is 70. An existing assertion-failure or signal status may be preserved for diagnostics, but is never evidence of a valid RED or completed lane result unless cleanup is independently `complete`. The status and command result are separate facts.

## Invocation shapes and ownership transitions

| Shape | State / sequence | Process-group, deadline, cancellation owner | Flock descriptor and mdpro lock | Cleanup-status owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Top level** | Validate configuration → wait for flock then mdpro → launch one new workload group with inherited flock FD → observe exit/drain or cancel/timeout → verify group and registered hosts → publish status → release owned mdpro directory and supervisor FD only if safe. | The top-level `heavy-lock.py` supervisor owns the new group, its launch-time deadline, TERM/KILL, and final drain verification. The caller may request cancellation; the supervisor executes it. | Supervisor acquires flock and passes its FD to the child/descendants. It acquires mdpro after flock unless disabled; only that acquirer may remove its mdpro directory after verified cleanup. | The top-level supervisor publishes its own invocation record after verification, independent of command exit code. |
| **2. Nested `PICSEE_HEAVY_LOCK_HELD=1` with inherited lock** | Parent launches protected group → nested launcher verifies an inherited lock FD and parent boundary/deadline handoff → child runs inside that boundary → nested work ends → verify only the nested invocation's workload → publish nested status; parent later verifies its whole group and publishes parent status. | The **parent** remains the sole owner of the containing process group, parent deadline, and cancellation. The nested supervisor owns only local observation/status. It must not make an independent untracked group, use a fresh deadline beyond the parent, or send a group signal that kills its ancestor. Parent cancellation reaches nested work, including TERM-resistant descendants, within the parent's grace. | Parent's flock FD is preserved and inherited by nested workload descendants. Nested code neither reacquires nor unlocks it. Parent owns any mdpro directory; nested code does not remove it. | Nested supervisor publishes only its invocation's verified result. It must not wait for the parent process or whole parent group to exit. If it cannot distinguish and verify its own remaining descendants, status is incomplete/unknown, not `complete`. Parent status is published only after parent-wide cleanup. |
| **3. CI `HELD=1` without inherited lock** | CI declares no local flock acquisition → an explicit supervisor creates/records the workload boundary before launch → runs the lane under a finite deadline → cancels/drains/verifies → publishes status. Missing supervisor is an error before launching a count-producing lane. | The explicit CI supervisor owns the group, deadline and cancellation; the GitHub job timeout is an outer backstop, not cleanup proof. `HELD=1` cannot select an unsupervised `exec` fast path. | There is no inherited flock descriptor and no claim of local lock exclusion. A status credential must identify the explicit supervisor, not fabricate an FD. | The explicit CI supervisor alone attests cleanup for that attempt. Lane output is rejected if its status is absent or unverified. |
| **4. Reverse nesting under mdpro (`MDPRO_HEAVY_LOCK_HELD=1`)** | mdpro parent already holds shared directory → picsee tries its flock without waiting behind a picsee holder that could be waiting on mdpro → launch protected group → drain/verify → publish local status. | mdpro owns its outer boundary and deadline; the picsee supervisor owns its local group and cancellation, subject to the mdpro parent's earlier deadline/cancellation. It must keep its group reachable by the mdpro boundary. A failed flock acquisition returns 75 without launching. | mdpro parent owns the shared directory and any inherited mdpro protection. Picsee acquires and passes its own flock FD; it never removes mdpro's directory. The flock stays held through its last protected descendant. | Picsee publishes a local invocation status when its own workload is verified; mdpro decides its outer cleanup separately. Local `complete` cannot attest the whole mdpro job. |
| **5. Hosted repeat** | For each iteration: repeat controller sets an iteration ID and remaining job deadline → explicit iteration supervisor starts worker group and registers hosted App processes → worker runs hosted/unhosted lane attempts → supervisor observes worker, group, hosts and helpers → publishes iteration status → controller starts next iteration only after `complete` and verified drain. | Iteration supervisor owns that worker group, host ownership, effective deadline and cancellation; repeat controller owns ordering and the overall job deadline. Inner lane attempts remain within the iteration boundary. Timeout/cancel reaches both group and registered hosted App processes. | If a real inherited flock exists, pass it through each protected worker and host path until cleanup. CI repeat with `HELD=1` and no flock uses the explicit iteration supervisor; no lock exclusion is claimed. Any mdpro directory belongs to its original acquirer. | Each lane attempt has its own local status. The iteration supervisor publishes a separate iteration status after group **and host** verification. The repeat controller consumes that status and never infers drain from worker exit or a prior iteration's record. |

**Nested status scope:** an inner lane can finish and report while its ancestor remains alive because it verifies its own invocation's descendants, not the entire containing group. The ancestor remains accountable for all work in its group. A nested `complete` does not permit the parent to release its locks, and a parent `complete` cannot be inferred from nested `complete`. If reliable local descendant identity cannot be maintained across reparenting or subprocess escape, the nested result is unverifiable. This rule deliberately leaves the tracking mechanism to phase 2; a process-group split is allowed only with explicit registration and parent cancellation reachability.

## Status publication and evidence gate

The supervision channel is separate from child stdout and stderr. Each attempt receives a fresh, unpredictable invocation ID and a single-use status destination owned by its supervisor. The child workload must not receive the status destination or authority to write it. The supervisor publishes only after its verification decision, using an atomic one-record write; the consumer binds the record to the expected invocation ID and supervisor identity, rejects a pre-existing/stale record, and reads it only after the publishing supervisor has finished its **local** verification. This ordering never waits for a nested lane's ancestor to exit. The channel must survive or explicitly report publication failure; a log line cannot substitute for it.

| Status value | Attestation | Lane treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `complete` | The named invocation's supervisor verified cleanup of every workload member it owns, including registered out-of-group hosts; the status is independent of whether tests passed or failed. | Only now may a complete, consistent test result count be accepted: pass → 0 with counts; completed assertion failure → 1 with counts and potentially a valid RED receipt. Timeout/cancellation never supplies assertion evidence even if cleanup completed. |
| `incomplete` | A survivor was seen, ownership was lost, verification failed/was inconclusive, or cleanup could not be completed. It never means test failure alone. | 70 without counts or valid RED. Preserve underlying exit/signal and survivor diagnostics separately. No next hosted repeat iteration. |
| `missing`, malformed, stale, contradictory, or unpublished | No trustworthy attestation for this invocation. These are consumer classifications, not positive supervisor assertions. | 70 without counts or valid RED. Do not infer `complete` from exit 0/1, output summaries, `HELD=1`, or stderr. |

A supervisor may publish `complete` after successfully cleaning up a timed-out or cancelled invocation, but the execution outcome remains timeout/cancel and the lane remains invalid for test counts/RED. A separate publication failure cannot be converted into `complete`. Every retry gets a new ID and status destination; one attempt's record cannot bless another. The same gate applies to Swift, App, hosted App and combined/repeat summaries. A combined lane reports counts only if every component it includes has a valid local attestation and complete result. A repeat controller reports a later iteration only after the prior iteration's own group/host cleanup is verified.

## #650 High failure modes excluded

| Parked round / audit finding | Failure | Contract exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Initial implementation, gpt F1 | A huge finite override overflowed wall-time formatting **after launch**, crashed the supervisor, and released the mdpro directory while the worker lived. | Validate representability and the monotonic deadline before acquiring/launching; errors after launch enter cancellation and verification, never a cleanup-success `finally` path. Unknown cleanup is `incomplete`; inherited flock is preserved. |
| First repair, gpt H1 (and initial opus F1's RED path) | A failing test with a live descendant could still exit 1 with counts; an unterminated descendant stderr prefix joined the text cleanup marker and hid it. | Cleanup is an independent, private, invocation-bound record. All lane/receipt gates require `complete`, including exit 1. Child stderr has no authority. |
| Status-file repair, opus round-c H1 | `HELD=1` directly `exec`ed the child, so CI App and hosted paths could not publish status and clean runs became 70. | Every `HELD=1` path has an explicit supervisor. Inherited-lock nesting and CI-without-lock are separate shapes; both publish their own verified status. |
| HELD supervisor repair, opus round-d H1 | A nested launcher made a fresh session/group and dropped the inherited FD; outer timeout/cancel killed only its own group, while nested descendants survived and the outer lock/status could appear clean. | Parent retains one cancellation boundary and its inherited FD reaches all protected descendants. Nested work stays in the parent group or is explicitly registered for parent cancellation; no inner deadline or grace delays the parent. Parent and nested cleanup proofs are scoped separately. |

## Open assumptions for implementation review

- The present code has no explicit inherited-FD credential or parent-deadline handoff for nested `HELD=1`. Phase 2 must choose a mechanism that cannot be forged by the flag alone and that preserves `pass_fds` across language runtimes. The exact descriptor number and transport are intentionally unspecified here.
- Current CI sets `PICSEE_HEAVY_LOCK_HELD=1` without acquiring a local flock. This contract treats the macOS job as single-tenant for local lock admission, as its workflow comment says; that does not prove cross-job exclusion. The explicit supervisor still has to verify process and hosted App cleanup.
- Current `app-repeat-process.mjs` already tracks a worker group and hosted processes, but its current `drained` JSON is not this contract's authenticated invocation status. Phase 4 must decide whether to adapt that supervisor or replace it.
- Current `heavy-lock.py` can inspect a process group with `killpg`/`ps`, but a nested local invocation needs a reliable way to distinguish its descendants from a live ancestor and siblings. No such mechanism was confirmed in current main. Until it exists, the nested status must fail closed. Process-group observation alone cannot prove that an escaped hosted App is gone.
- A reverse-nested mdpro parent may have a deadline and cancellation boundary outside picsee's control. No authenticated mdpro-to-picsee deadline handoff was confirmed in current main; phase 2 must either provide one or treat the outer mdpro boundary as a separately enforced backstop and report that limitation.
